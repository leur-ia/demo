# Pinholing

Tiny holes in the surface of a fired glaze, as if someone went over it with a needle. Sometimes they are little craters with smooth edges, sometimes they go right through to the clay.

They come from gas. As the kiln heats, the clay and glaze give off gases from organic matter, from carbonates breaking down, from sulphur in the clay. The bubbles rise through the melting glaze and burst at the surface. If the glaze is too stiff or the firing ends too soon, the craters don't have time to flow back together.

My checklist:

1. Is the bisque hot enough? A bisque below cone 06 leaves too much organic matter in the clay.
2. Is the glaze too thick? Thick coats trap more gas.
3. Was there a soak at the top? Fifteen minutes lets the surface heal.
4. Was the reduction too heavy early on? Carbon trapped in the body can bubble through later.

I also rub the dry glaze surface lightly with a fingertip before firing, which closes the powdery holes that sometimes form over porous spots.

Dark, fluid glazes like [[Tenmoku]] hide it; pale ones like [[Celadon]] show every hole. A soak in the schedule (see [[Firing schedules and cones]]) cured most of mine, together with a hotter bisque in the [[Electric kiln]].
