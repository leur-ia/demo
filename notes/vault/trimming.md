# Trimming

Trimming is where a pot gets its foot and its lightness. I wait until the pot is leather hard, firm enough to hold its shape upside down but still soft enough that a fingernail leaves a mark, then centre it upside down on the wheel and hold it with three small lumps of clay.

My tools are simple: a looped steel trimming tool, a sharpened hacksaw blade for the outside curve, and a wooden rib to burnish at the end. I tap the base while it spins; a dull thud means too thick, a hollow knock means it's close.

What I'm aiming for:

- a wall that is the same thickness from rim to foot;
- a foot ring that lifts the pot a few millimetres off the table and throws a small shadow;
- no sharp inner corner where the floor meets the wall, because that's where [[S-cracks]] like to begin.

For [[Tea bowls]] I trim much more freely, sometimes leaving the spiral of the tool marks showing and cutting the foot slightly off-centre. For [[Lids and galleries]] trimming is exact work with calipers.

If the clay chatters under the tool, it's too dry. A spray of water and ten minutes under plastic usually saves it. [[Porcelain]] gives me a much narrower window than stoneware, so I check it every hour.
