# Lids and galleries

A lid that rattles, sticks or sits crooked will make someone put a jar back on the table. I measure everything.

When I throw a lidded jar I make a **gallery**: a small ledge inside the rim for the lid to rest on. I measure its width with calipers straight away, while the jar is still on the wheel, and I throw the lid in the same session from the same clay so they shrink together.

My routine:

1. Throw the jar with a gallery about 5 mm wide.
2. Set the calipers to the inside of the gallery.
3. Throw the lid upside down, like a shallow bowl, and check it against the calipers.
4. Trim both at the same stage of dryness, then test the fit before bisque.

In the glaze firing, lid and jar go into the kiln together, lid in place, with the contact surfaces wiped clean of glaze. A dab of alumina wash on the gallery stops them welding.

The trickiest is the lid on [[A teapot that pours well]], because it needs a flange deep enough not to fall out when you tilt the pot. For jars in [[Porcelain]], I always fire the lid in place to stop the rim warping out of round. See [[Trimming]] for how I finish the knob.
