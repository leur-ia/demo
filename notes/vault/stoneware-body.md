# Stoneware body

Almost everything I make starts from the same bag: a buff stoneware that matures at cone 10, around 1280 °C. It has about 15% fine grog, which gives it tooth on the wheel and keeps big forms from slumping. Fired in reduction it goes a warm, toasty grey, and where the glaze thins over an edge you get a rusty flash from the iron in the clay.

I chose it for three reasons:

- it forgives me when I throw thin walls and then leave them in a draught;
- it takes [[Celadon]] and [[Tenmoku]] beautifully, because the iron speckles through;
- shrinkage is a manageable 12%, so my lids still fit after firing.

It is not a pretty clay to handle raw. It dries patchy, and the grog can be harsh on the fingertips after a long day of [[Throwing on the wheel]]. For small, delicate work I switch to [[Porcelain]], but for mugs, bowls and anything that has to survive a dishwasher, this is the body I trust.

A fresh bag always gets a proper session of [[Wedging]] before it touches the wheel, even though the supplier swears it's de-aired. Old habits, and I've had enough hidden pockets burst in the kiln to keep them.

> A clay body is a promise about how the pot will behave in the fire. Learn one well before you flirt with ten.
