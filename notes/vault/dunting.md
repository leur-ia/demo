# Dunting

The kiln was down to about 200 °C, and I was sitting outside with a cup of tea when I heard it: a clean, bright ping from inside, like a spoon tapped on a glass. The next day I found a large bowl split in two, one clean line running from rim to foot, the edges sharp and glassy as if it had been cut with a blade.

That's **dunting**: the pot has torn itself apart from stress during cooling, or sometimes heating. The line is the giveaway. A crack from cooling has sharp edges that the glaze hasn't flowed into, whereas a crack that opened while the glaze was still molten has rounded, healed edges.

The main culprits:

- **Cooling too fast** through the silica inversions at 573 °C and 226 °C, when quartz in the clay suddenly contracts. Opening the damper or the door early is enough.
- **Uneven walls**: a thick base and a thin rim cool at different speeds and pull against each other.
- **Glaze on one side only**, which puts the pot under tension. A bowl glazed thickly inside and bare outside is a classic.
- **Cold air** from a draught hitting one side of the pot.

Remedies: keep the damper closed until 150 °C, throw walls evenly (see [[Throwing on the wheel]]), and glaze insides and outsides to similar thicknesses. My cooling notes live in [[Firing schedules and cones]].

It's a different fault from [[S-cracks]], which begin in the base during drying.
