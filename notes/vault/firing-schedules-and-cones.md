# Firing schedules and cones

A thermocouple tells you the temperature of the air. A cone tells you what the heat has actually done to the pots. I trust cones.

**Pyrometric cones** are slender pyramids of ceramic material designed to bend at a known combination of temperature and time. I put a pack of three in front of each spy hole: cone 9, 10 and 11. Cone 9 going down is my warning; cone 10 bent over until its tip touches the base means the glaze is mature, around 1280 °C; cone 11 should only just be leaning.

My glaze firing in outline:

1. 100 °C/h to 600 °C.
2. Full speed to 1000 °C, then reduction in the [[Gas kiln reduction|gas kiln]].
3. 60–80 °C/h through the last 100 °C to cone 10.
4. A 15-minute soak to let the glaze smooth out and heal [[Pinholing]].

The cooling half matters as much as the climb. I let the kiln fall quickly from top temperature to about 1000 °C, then hold it back through the crystal range, which is good for [[Tenmoku]]. Around 573 °C and 226 °C, silica in the clay changes size suddenly (the quartz and cristobalite inversions), so I keep the damper closed and resist opening anything. Opening the door early for a look is how pots split; see [[Dunting]].

I unload below 100 °C, never earlier, however tempted I am.
