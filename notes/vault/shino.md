# Shino

Shino is the glaze I keep going back to even though it rarely behaves. It's mostly feldspar with a bit of clay and very little flux, and it fires to a thick, fatty, milky surface that ranges from white to orange to a smoky grey, all on the same pot.

The grey patches are called **carbon trap**. When the kiln goes into reduction early, carbon gets caught in the glaze before it melts, and it stays there as soft charcoal clouds. It's unpredictable, which is the whole appeal.

What makes it difficult:

- it shrinks a lot as it dries, so applied thick it splits into a mosaic and then draws back into bald patches in the fire (I've written about that under [[Crawling]]);
- it needs early, heavy reduction in the [[Gas kiln reduction|gas kiln]];
- it doesn't like the [[Electric kiln]] at all; it just goes a dull biscuit colour.

I add a little soda ash to my version, which helps the carbon trap and makes the orange blush warmer where the glaze is thin.

Shino looks best on heavy, generous forms: thick-walled [[Tea bowls]], water jars. On a delicate cup it looks like a coat that's too big.
