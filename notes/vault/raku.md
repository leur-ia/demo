# Raku

Raku is the opposite of everything patient about my usual firings. Once or twice a summer I set up a small fibre-lined kiln in the yard, fire it with propane, and pull pots out glowing orange at about 950 °C with long tongs.

The sequence goes like this:

1. Load bisqued pots coated in a low-fire raku glaze.
2. Fire fast, about 45 minutes, until the glaze looks wet and shiny through the spy hole.
3. Lift the pot out with tongs and drop it into a metal bin of sawdust and newspaper.
4. The paper bursts into flame. Put the lid on and let it smoulder for fifteen minutes.
5. Pull it out, plunge it in water, scrub off the soot.

The smoky, oxygen-starved bin blackens any bare clay and pulls copper glazes into metallic reds and purples. The shock of the cold air makes the glaze form a wide web of lines, which the smoke then stains black: [[Crazing]] as decoration instead of a fault.

The body has to be open and heavily grogged to survive the thermal shock; my regular [[Stoneware body]] copes if the walls are even. Thin, fine clay would split instantly, the same way the pots in [[Dunting]] give way when cold air reaches them.

Raku pots aren't watertight, and I never sell them for food. They go to [[The Saturday market]] as decorative pieces.
