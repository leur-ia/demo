# Wedging

Before any clay goes near the wheel, I knead it on the canvas-covered bench. Forty or fifty turns, rocking the heel of my hands forward and letting the lump fold back on itself. It looks like making bread, and it does much the same job: it evens out the moisture and pushes trapped pockets of air to the surface where they can escape.

I use two methods depending on the batch:

1. **Ram's head** for small amounts: push down and forward with both palms, lift, rotate slightly, repeat.
2. **Spiral** for bigger lumps: one hand drives down while the other turns the clay, so it builds up a shape like a seashell.

To check my work I cut the lump in half with a wire and look at the faces. If I see little holes or streaks of softer clay, it goes back for another round.

It matters most with [[Reclaiming clay|reclaimed clay]], which comes off the plaster wet on one side and stiff on the other. Skip it and the lump wobbles on the wheel, or worse, a hidden pocket expands in the bisque and blows out a chunk of wall. [[Throwing on the wheel]] is so much easier when the lump is honest all the way through.

It's also my warm-up. Ten minutes at the bench and my shoulders have loosened, which the whole [[Studio routine]] depends on.
