# Ash glaze

Every winter I save the ash from the wood stove in a metal bin. By spring there's enough to make a bucket of glaze.

The oldest recipe is almost laughably simple: equal parts wood ash and clay. The ash is full of calcium, potassium and a little phosphorus, which melts the clay into glass at cone 10. The result is a runny, streaky surface that drips down the pot in little rivulets and gathers in pools of glassy olive and amber.

Preparing it:

1. Soak the ash in water for a day and pour off the liquid.
2. Sieve it through an 80-mesh screen to get rid of charcoal and nails.
3. Dry it, weigh it, then mix it with the clay and water.

Each batch is different because each tree is different. Apple wood from the neighbour's orchard gave me a paler, milkier glaze than the oak did. I run [[Test tiles]] for every new bin, never trusting last year's results.

It's the closest I can get in the gas kiln to the look of [[Wood firing]], where the ash lands on the pots from the flame itself. Wet ash is caustic and dry ash is dusty, so washing it is done outside with gloves and a mask, as in [[Looking after hands and lungs]].
