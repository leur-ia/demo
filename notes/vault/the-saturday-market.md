# The Saturday market

Every Saturday from April to December I pack the van at six and set up my stall in the square by the covered hall. Two trestle tables, a linen cloth, three wooden crates turned on end for height, and a hand-lettered sign.

What I've learned about selling there:

- **Group by use.** Mugs together, bowls together, [[Tenmoku]] and pale glazes kept apart so each looks its best.
- **Let people touch.** The moment someone picks up a mug, the handle does the selling.
- **Keep a jug of tea brewed** in one of my own pots, and offer cups. It opens conversations.
- **Bring seconds.** A basket of slightly flawed pieces at half price draws in people who would never buy a full-priced jar, and some come back for one later.

A good day is forty or fifty pots and a dozen useful conversations. A wet day in November can be six mugs and frozen fingers.

The market is also my best research. I see which shapes vanish in the first hour and which sit all day, which changes what I make the following week. It's where most of my [[Commissions]] begin: someone asks, "Could you make eight of these?"

I pack up at two, tally sales in the notebook, and adjust [[Pricing my work|my prices]] only once a year. The takings from one good Saturday are in [[Journal — 7 June]].
