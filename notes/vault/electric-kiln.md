# Electric kiln

The electric kiln is the unglamorous workhorse. A 100-litre top-loader in the corner of the studio, it does all my bisque firings and the occasional oxidation glaze load.

Bisque is where I'm most careful. I fire to cone 04, around 1060 °C, and the early stage is gentle: 60 °C per hour up to 600 °C, with the lid propped open a finger's width until 500 °C so the steam and burning organics can escape. Rushing that part is how you blow out a thick base or get [[Dunting]] in the bisque.

In oxidation, with plenty of air and no flame to starve, glazes behave very differently from the [[Gas kiln reduction|gas kiln]]:

- iron glazes go amber and honey rather than blue-grey;
- colours are cleaner and more predictable;
- [[Shino]] and carbon trap effects simply don't happen.

I use a digital controller with saved programmes, and I still put witness cones on the shelf because the thermocouple drifts as it ages; see [[Firing schedules and cones]].

Maintenance, twice a year:

1. Vacuum out the element grooves, mask on.
2. Check the elements' resistance with a meter.
3. Replace the thermocouple every hundred firings or so.

It's also the kiln I use for [[Test tiles]] when I need an answer fast.
