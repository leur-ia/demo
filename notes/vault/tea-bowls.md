# Tea bowls

A tea bowl, a chawan, is the pot that taught me the most. It looks simple: a bowl a little bigger than two cupped hands, no handle. But everything about it is judged by the hands and lips of the person drinking.

What I try to get right:

- **Weight**: heavier than it looks, but never heavy. About 350–400 g.
- **Rim**: slightly uneven, soft to the lips, turning inward a little so the tea doesn't slop when whisked.
- **Foot**: cut with care; it's the first thing a guest turns over to look at.
- **Inside**: a small well at the bottom where the last of the whisked tea gathers.

I throw them from softer clay, off the hump, and leave some of the making visible: a finger groove, a twist in the wall. [[Trimming]] is where each one gets its character; I cut the foot freely and sometimes leave the tool marks.

My favourites come out of [[Wood firing]] or glazed in [[Shino]], which gives a warm, thick skin that feels good held in both hands. A [[Tenmoku]] rim with a rusty edge is my second choice.

They're also the pots I sell least readily, which is fine. I bring them to [[Tea mornings]] and let people hold them before they decide. Nobody chooses a tea bowl with their eyes alone.
