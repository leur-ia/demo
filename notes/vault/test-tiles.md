# Test tiles

I make about a hundred test tiles every month. They're small L-shaped pieces of my [[Stoneware body]], about 5 cm tall, with a stamped number on the back and a bit of texture pressed into the front so I can see how the glaze breaks over edges.

The standing L-shape matters: the vertical part shows whether a glaze runs, the horizontal foot shows how it pools.

My method for a new glaze:

1. Mix a 100 g batch, weighing everything to a tenth of a gram.
2. Dip each tile in two thicknesses: once, then again on the top half.
3. Write the recipe, the tile number and the position in the kiln in the test notebook.
4. Fire them in the next glaze load, spread around hot and cool spots.

For line blends, I mix two versions of a glaze and combine them in steps of 10%, which quickly shows me where a colour goes wrong. That's how I found the iron level for [[Celadon]].

It's tedious and it's the most important work in the studio. Every expensive disaster I've had came from skipping it: a whole firing of mugs with [[Crawling]], a batch of bowls that crazed a week after they sold. See [[Glaze chemistry basics]] for how I read the results.
