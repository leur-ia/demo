# Pulling handles

A mug lives or dies by its handle. People pick it up at the stall and within a second they've decided whether it belongs in their hand.

I pull handles straight from the mug rather than attaching a pre-made strap. The method:

1. Score and slip the side of a leather-hard mug where the top of the handle will join.
2. Press a carrot-shaped lump of clay firmly onto it.
3. Hold the mug sideways, wet my hand, and stroke the clay downward, squeezing gently, until it thins into a strap with a slight ridge down the middle.
4. Bend it into a loop and press the end on lower down, leaving room for three fingers.

The handle must dry at the same speed as the body, or it'll pull away at the joint. I wrap the mugs loosely in plastic for a day, then leave them upside down to firm up.

Things I check:

- the thumb has a natural place to rest at the top;
- the lower join doesn't trap glaze in a thick puddle, which can give me [[Crazing]] or a nasty drip;
- the curve echoes the profile of the mug, which I shaped while [[Throwing on the wheel]].

The same skills go into the handle of [[A teapot that pours well]], where balance matters even more.
