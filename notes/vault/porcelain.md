# Porcelain

Porcelain is the clay I love and resent in equal measure. It is smooth as cold butter, has almost no grog, and at cone 10 it goes white and faintly translucent where the wall is thin. Hold a thin bowl up to a window and you can see the shadow of your fingers through it.

The price is that it has no patience. It remembers every mistake:

- a wall thrown unevenly will warp in the kiln, often into a lazy oval;
- a base left too thick will give me [[S-cracks]];
- a rim that dries faster than the foot will split before it's even bisqued.

So I throw it wetter and faster than my [[Stoneware body]], and I cover pots loosely with plastic so they dry evenly. [[Trimming]] has to happen at exactly leather hard; a few hours late and the tool chatters across the surface.

Glazes look completely different on it. My [[Celadon]] pools on porcelain to an icy blue that the stoneware never gives me, because there is so little iron in the clay to muddy it. I keep porcelain for tea bowls, small cups and the occasional lidded jar, never for the big work.
