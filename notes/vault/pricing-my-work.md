# Pricing my work

For years I priced by looking at what other people asked and going a bit lower. It left me exhausted and barely covering the gas bill.

Now I work it out properly. For each type of pot I add up:

1. **Materials**: clay, glaze, a share of kiln shelves and the fuel for two firings. For a mug, about €3.
2. **Time**: throwing, trimming, handle, glazing, packing, divided by the batch size. Around 40 minutes per mug, paid at an hourly rate I'd be happy to earn.
3. **Overheads**: rent, insurance, the market stall fee, spread across everything I sell in a year.
4. **Losses**: roughly one in eight pots doesn't survive the kiln or comes out with a fault. The good ones have to pay for them.

That gives me a wholesale figure, which I roughly double for retail, and then I ask whether the number feels right. If it's too high, I simplify the form rather than cutting the price.

Mugs are €28, tea bowls from €60, large jars from €180. Seconds with small faults go on a separate shelf at half price, and I'm honest about what's wrong with them.

The hardest thing was accepting that underpricing isn't generous. It undercuts other makers and tells people handmade work is worth less. Made-to-order work is costed differently again, with a deposit; see [[Commissions]]. What sells at [[The Saturday market]] tells me whether I got it right.
