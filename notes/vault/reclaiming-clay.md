# Reclaiming clay

Nothing clay-shaped leaves this studio as waste until it's been fired. Trimmings, collapsed cylinders, the slurry at the bottom of the splash pan: all of it goes into the reclaim bucket under the sink.

My process takes about a week:

1. Let the scraps dry out completely, bone dry, so they break down evenly when wet.
2. Drop them into a bucket of water and leave them to slake overnight. Dry clay falls apart into a soft sludge on its own.
3. Pour off the clear water and stir the sludge smooth.
4. Spread it two fingers thick on a big plaster slab and wait. The plaster drinks the water out from underneath.
5. When it lifts off the plaster cleanly and no longer sticks to my palm, it's ready.

Then it needs a very thorough [[Wedging]], because the top and bottom stiffen at different speeds.

I never mix [[Porcelain]] scraps into the stoneware bucket; one speck of dark grog in a white pot is enough to ruin it. The two have separate buckets with big painted labels.

Reclaim is also where the dust comes from, if I'm careless. Dried scraps get handled wet or not at all, and I wipe the bench down with a sponge rather than sweeping. See [[Looking after hands and lungs]].

There's something satisfying about it. A mug that failed in March can come back as a tea bowl in April, thrown again on the [[Throwing on the wheel|wheel]].
