# Gas kiln reduction

The gas kiln sits under the lean-to behind the studio: a 0.7 m³ downdraft with two burners, built from soft brick and old kiln shelves. It's the heart of my stoneware.

**Reduction** means starving the flame of oxygen. With the damper pushed in, the burners can't get enough air to burn cleanly, so the flame goes hunting for oxygen in the clay and glazes themselves. That's what turns the iron in [[Celadon]] blue-grey and gives my [[Stoneware body]] its toasty flashing.

My usual firing:

1. Climb in oxidation to about 1000 °C, with cone 010 bending.
2. Push the damper in until a lazy yellow flame licks from the spy holes and there's a faint smell of smoke. That's **body reduction**, held for about 40 minutes.
3. Ease back to a lighter reduction and climb to cone 10, around 1280 °C, over the next five hours.
4. Clear the kiln with a short oxidation at the end, then shut everything down and close the damper.

Too heavy a reduction and the glazes go dull and trapped carbon can cause bloating and [[Pinholing]]. Too light and the celadon comes out a sad yellow.

I keep a notebook of every firing: gas pressure, damper position, cone times. It's the only way to repeat a good result. See [[Firing schedules and cones]] for how I read the cones through the spy hole.
