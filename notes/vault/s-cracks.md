# S-cracks

An S-crack is a small, curling line in the floor of a pot, shaped like a stretched letter S. Usually it's in the centre of a bowl or a mug, and usually it shows up after the bisque or the glaze firing, though it began long before.

It begins on the wheel. When I open a lump and pull the floor outward, the clay particles in the middle stay loosely arranged in a spiral, while the walls get tightly compressed by my pulls. As the pot dries, the base shrinks unevenly along that spiral, and it opens.

How I prevent it:

1. Compress the floor with a finger or a rib, moving from the centre outward, two or three times while throwing.
2. Sponge out standing water from the bottom; a pool of slurry softens the base.
3. Don't leave the floor much thicker than the walls.
4. Dry pots upside down once they're firm, so the base doesn't dry last.
5. When [[Trimming]], avoid leaving a thin foot around a thick centre.

[[Porcelain]] is especially prone because its fine particles line up so easily. A compressed base is the single habit that removed most of them from my work, which is why I lecture everyone who comes to watch me at [[Throwing on the wheel|the wheel]].

It's often confused with [[Dunting]], but an S-crack is a drying fault in the base, not a cooling fault in the wall.
