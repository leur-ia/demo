# Tenmoku

If celadon is water, tenmoku is black tea. It's a high-iron glaze (mine has 10% red iron oxide) that fires to a deep, glossy black-brown and breaks to a rusty amber wherever it thins over a rim or a ridge.

I love it on the outside of mugs and on the rim of [[Tea bowls]], where the lip turns a toasted orange. In a good firing, tiny oil-spot crystals form on the surface as the iron comes out of solution during the cooling.

Notes to myself:

1. Apply it moderately. Too thick and it runs down onto the kiln shelf; too thin and it goes a dull brown.
2. It likes a gentle cool through 1000–900 °C. That's where the rust and the crystals develop; see [[Firing schedules and cones]].
3. Double dipping over [[Celadon]] on the top third of a pot gives a lovely soft transition.

It's very forgiving of [[Pinholing]] because its dark, fluid surface heals over. People at [[The Saturday market]] reach for the tenmoku mugs first nearly every week, which tells me something about what they actually want at home.
