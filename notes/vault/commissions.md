# Commissions

About a third of my income now comes from pieces made to order. A family wanting twelve plates in one glaze for a wedding anniversary; a café that needs fifty cups that stack; a woman who wanted a lidded jar in the colour of the sea near her childhood home.

I love this work and I've learned to be careful with it. My process:

1. **A conversation first**, ideally at the studio, with pots in hand. People often don't know what they want until they hold something.
2. **Sketches and a sample**, fired, so they can see the actual clay and glaze.
3. **A written agreement**: quantity, sizes, glaze, delivery date, and a 50% deposit up front.
4. **Make extra.** For twelve plates I throw eighteen, because the kiln always takes its share.
5. **Photos along the way**, so there are no surprises at delivery.

What I tell everyone: handmade means each piece is a sibling, not a twin. Colours drift between firings, especially [[Celadon]], which depends on the reduction in the [[Gas kiln reduction|gas kiln]].

I say no to things I can't do well, and to anything with a deadline too tight for a proper test firing. The time and materials sums in [[Pricing my work]] apply, plus a bit more for the extra conversations and the risk.

Many of them begin at [[The Saturday market]] or over a bowl of tea at one of the [[Tea mornings]].
