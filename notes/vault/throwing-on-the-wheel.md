# Throwing on the wheel

Throwing starts with centering, and centering is most of the battle. I slap a ball of about 500 g onto the wheel head, wet it, brace my left elbow against my thigh and lean into the clay with my whole body rather than my arms. The lump rises into a cone under my palms, gets pushed back down, and after two or three rounds it stops fighting. When it's true, it feels like a still point in the middle of a spinning world, and my hands don't move at all.

Then:

1. Open with the thumbs down to about 1 cm above the wheel head.
2. Pull the floor out, compressing it with a finger so it doesn't give me [[S-cracks]] later.
3. Make three pulls up the wall, each one thinner, with the wheel easing off as the wall rises.
4. Collar in, shape, and finish the rim with a strip of chamois.

Most of my faults are made in the first minute. A lump that wasn't well prepared by [[Wedging]] never settles; a wall that's thicker on one side will twist in the kiln.

Once the pot is off the wheel, it waits under plastic until it's firm enough for [[Trimming]]. Mugs then go on to get handles (see [[Pulling handles]]), jars wait for their lids.

> Don't chase the clay. Let it come to your hands.
